# Atrial Fibrillation, care instructions

## What is wrong?

- Atrial fibrillation is an irregular and often fast heartbeat which can cause different problems
- It can be the cause of blood clots that can travel from your heart to your brain and bring on a stroke
- It can lead to chest pain or heart failure
- It can cause fast heart rates (palpitations) that can make you weak, dizzy or short of breath

## Care at home?

- Treatment can help you feel better and prevent future problems. The treatment will probably be pills that you take every day. Treatment can slow the heart rate, control the heart rhythm, or help prevent stroke.
- When in the Emergency room you may have had a procedure, such as electrical cardioversion or intravenous medication to bring your atrial fibrillation under control
- Take your medicines exactly as prescribed. Call your doctor or nurse advice line if you think you are having a problem with your medicine.
- The pills you are prescribed you may need to be on every day
- Your doctor may have given you a blood thinner to prevent a stroke, take these medications exactly as prescribed
- Live a healthy lifestyle, do not smoke, exercise, eat healthy foods, sleep 7 – 9 hours per night and if you drink alcohol limit your intake to 2 drinks a week

## When should I feel better?

- The medicine you are taking and any treatment you got in the emergency room should make you feel better in a few days, but it will be important to take your medicine without stopping
- Call your doctor or nurse advice line if you think you are having a problem with your medicine.

## Go to a walk-in clinic or the Emergency Department right away if:

- If you are having problems with chest pain, shortness of breath, fast heart rate (palpitations) or if you feel you need to have your heart checked
- You can call the 811 line if you aren’t sure if you need to go in or not
- The emergency physicians and nurses are always here to give you support

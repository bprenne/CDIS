# Asthma Exacerbation

## What is wrong?

- Asthma happens when your airways (tubes in your chest that carry air to your lungs) swell and narrow, making it hard to breathe.
- Things that can trigger your asthma include: an infection (cold or flu), allergies, irritants (such as smoke, perfume, pollution), heavy exercise, cold air
- Symptoms include being short of breath, chest tightness, cough and wheezing.
- You can help prevent these attacks by keeping your asthma under control and treating symptoms before they get bad.

## What to expect at home?

- People with asthma have symptoms that come and go throughout their life. There can be times when your symptoms are worse than usual, and treatment is needed to stop these flare-ups from happening.
- Main symptoms of asthma are: trouble breathing, wheezing (a whistling sound when breathing) coughing, tight feeling in the chest
- Take your asthma medicines as prescribed, use your quick-relief (rescue) inhaler when you have symptoms of an asthma attack, salbutamol (blue inhaler) is the quick relief medicine that is often used.
- Other inhalers are to be used every day to control your symptom, with the goal to prevent problems before they occur
- If your doctor prescribed corticosteroid pills to use during an attack, take them exactly as prescribed. It may take hours for the pills to work, but they may make the episode shorter and help you breathe better.
- Keep your quick relief (rescue) inhaler with you at all times

## When should I feel better?

- You should feel better over the next few days as you use your regular medications and any new medications the doctor has prescribed for you
- Many things can make your asthma worse, never smoke and avoid any exposure to smoke
- Learn what triggers an asthma attack for you and avoid those triggers. Common triggers include, colds, smoke, dust, pollen stress and cold air

## Go to a walk-in clinic or the Emergency Department right away if:

- You have severe symptoms of shortness of breath or coughing, wheezing and/or chest tightness
- You have shortness of breath while resting or can’t speak in full sentences
- You symptoms don’t get better after using your puffer or you need to use your puffer more than every 4 hours
- You have a new or higher fever

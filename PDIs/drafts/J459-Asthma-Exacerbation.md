# Asthma Exacerbation (Asthma Attack)

## What is wrong?
- Your airways swelled and got narrow, so breathing is hard.
- Colds, flu, allergies, smoke, exercise, or cold air can start attacks.
- You may cough, wheeze, feel short of breath, or have a tight chest.
- Keeping your asthma under control helps prevent attacks.

## What should I do at home?
- Take your asthma medicines exactly as prescribed.
- Use your blue rescue inhaler (salbutamol) when symptoms start.
- Keep your rescue inhaler with you at all times.
- Use your daily inhaler every day, even when you feel well.
- If you got steroid pills, take them exactly as prescribed.

## When should I feel better?
- You should feel better over the next few days.
- Never smoke, and stay away from smoke.
- Learn what starts your attacks, and avoid those things.

## See a doctor if:
- See your family doctor soon to review your asthma plan.

## Go to the Emergency Department right away if:
- You have bad shortness of breath, cough, wheeze, or chest tightness.
- You are short of breath at rest or cannot speak full sentences.
- Your puffer does not help, or you need it more than every 4 hours.
- You have a new or higher fever.

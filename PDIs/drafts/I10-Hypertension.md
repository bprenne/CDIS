# Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

## What is wrong?
- Your blood pressure stays higher than normal.
- It usually has no symptoms, like headaches or dizziness.
- Over time, it raises your risk of stroke and heart attack.
- Very high blood pressure (180/110 or higher) can harm your brain, heart, eyes, and kidneys.
- In the Emergency Department, you may have had medicine to lower it.

## What should I do at home?
- Take your blood pressure medicine exactly as prescribed.
- Learn to check your blood pressure at home. Write down each reading.
- Be active, eat less salt, and keep a healthy weight.
- Do not smoke. Smoking raises your risk of heart attack and stroke.

## When should I feel better?
- Most people with high blood pressure do not feel sick.
- Your home readings help your doctor choose the right medicine dose.

## See a doctor if:
- See your family doctor as often as they tell you.
- Ask your doctor what your blood pressure goal should be.
- You think you are having a problem with your medicine.

## Go to the Emergency Department right away if:
- Your blood pressure is 180/110 or higher, even without symptoms.
- You have a bad headache or blurry vision.
- You have chest pain or pressure, sweating, nausea, or trouble breathing.
- You have pain in your back, neck, jaw, belly, shoulders, or arms.
- Your face, arm, or leg is suddenly weak, numb, or droopy.
- You suddenly have trouble talking, seeing, walking, or thinking.
- You feel light-headed, or your heartbeat is fast or uneven.

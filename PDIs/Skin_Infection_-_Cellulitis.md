# Skin Infection (Cellulitis)

## What is wrong?

- Cellulitis is a skin infection caused by bacteria, most often strep or staph. It often occurs after a break in the skin from a scrape, cut, bite, or puncture, or after a rash.
- Cellulitis may be treated without doing tests to find out what caused it. But your doctor may do tests, if needed, to look for a specific bacteria, like methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*(MRSA).
- Cellulitis can cause skin redness, swelling, pain, warmth, rash, fever chills, nausea and vomiting

## Care at home?

- Take your antibiotics as directed. Do not stop taking them just because you feel better. You need to take the full course of antibiotics.
- If your infection is on your arm or leg prop up the infected area on pillows to reduce pain and swelling.
- Avoid pools and hot tubs during treatment
- Rest the area as best you can
- If there is drainage keep the area covered with a dressing suggested by your caregiver

## When should I feel better?

- The antibiotics need time to work, it may take two to three days before the infection looks better, it may take up to a week for it to go away
- Take your antibiotics as directed. Do not stop taking them just because you feel better. You need to take the full course of antibiotics.

## Ways to prevent cellulitis

- Cellulitis most often occurs where there is a break in the skin. Try to prevent cuts, scrapes, or other injuries to your skin
- Clean any break in the skin with soap and water and protect with a bandage
- If you have diabetes examine your feet and legs daily for breaks in the skin.
- Don’t use hydrogen peroxide or alcohol, which can slow healing

## Go to a walk-in clinic or the Emergency Department right away if:

- There are signs that your infection is getting worse, such as:
- Increased pain, swelling, warmth or redness
- Red streaks leading up from the area or pus
- Fever over 38 C, chills or sweats
- You are feeling sick over all, with nausea and weakness
- If you are not getting better after 2 or 3 days
